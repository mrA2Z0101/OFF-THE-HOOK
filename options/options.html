Detection mode

Rule-based checks always run, entirely inside your browser, with no setup. AI analysis is optional and sends a copy of the open email to the provider you choose below.

Off · rule-based only

When on, every email you open is sent (after the privacy filtering below) to your AI provider. Off by default.

AI provider

Install Ollama, run ollama pull llama3.1, then enter the model name. If the test fails with HTTP 403, see the README section on OLLAMA_ORIGINS.

Examples: https://api.openai.com/v1, https://openrouter.ai/api/v1, http://localhost:1234/v1 (LM Studio).

For example claude-sonnet-5-5, or claude-haiku-4-5 for lower cost.

AI options

Sensitivity

Your company

With this set, Off the Hook will:

  • Warn about possibly compromised coworker accounts. If an email from your own domain asks for your phone number, passwords or personal details, gift cards, money transfers or bank details, or a secret favor, or contains a deceptive link or dangerous attachment, you'll see a "Possibly compromised coworker account" warning telling you to check with the person in person before doing anything.
  • Catch outsiders pretending to be your company, such as look-alike domains (yourcompany-payroll.com, yourcornpany.com) or your company's name in the sender name of an outside or Gmail address.

This does not make your company's email trusted. Everyday requests between coworkers ("text me when the truck arrives", "are you free at 2?") don't trigger the warning.

Trusted senders and domains

Mail from these senders gets a much lower score (unless sender authentication fails), and links to trusted domains skip look-alike checks. Attackers can fake a sender's name and address, so add only domains you are sure about.

Custom keywords

The built-in phrase lists are in data/keywords.json inside the extension folder. Edit that file and reload the extension to change them (see README).

Your reports

When you click It's phishing or It's safe in the Off the Hook panel on an email, the report is saved here, on this computer only, and Off the Hook learns from it right away.

  • It's phishing: future email from that sender, from that sender's domain (unless it's a shared one like gmail.com), or linking to the same sites gets flagged. Phrases you selected ("What gave it away?") are watched for.
  • It's safe: the warnings that fired are quieted for that sender. Compromised-coworker, failed-authentication and other critical warnings always stay on.
  • A later report about the same sender replaces an earlier one. Delete a report to undo what was learned from it.

Known scam sites

Once a day, Off the Hook downloads free public lists of active phishing and malware sites and checks every link (and sender) against them on this computer. Only the lists are downloaded: nothing about your email is ever sent.

    Turning a list off takes effect after you save (the lists are rebuilt then). Official brand sites, your company, your trusted senders and big shared services (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Dropbox, GitHub…) are never flagged just for being listed.

    Contact memory

    Off the Hook remembers who you've had normal email from, so it can warn you when a familiar name writes from an address they've never used with you (a favourite trick in boss and coworker scams), or when someone you've never heard from asks for something.

    • Names and addresses are stored only as scrambled fingerprints on this computer. Only the usual domain for a name (like yourcompany.com) is kept readable, for the warning text.
    • An address counts as known after three normal emails on different days, or as soon as you click It's safe. Clicking It's phishing forgets the sender.
    • "First email from this sender" notes start once 40 normal emails have been learned.

    Highlight preferences

    Severity is also shown by underline style (solid, dashed, dotted), a ⚠ icon for high risk, and an "AI" tag for AI highlights, so colors are never the only signal.

    Privacy and data

    • Rule-based mode: your email never leaves your browser. The only downloads are the public scam-site lists (once a day, if turned on); no email data is sent with them.
    • AI mode: for each email you open, Off the Hook sends the sender name and address, reply-to, subject, body (first 3,000 characters unless "full body" is on), link text and addresses, attachment file names, and the rule findings to the provider you configured. With redaction on, email addresses keep only their domain ([email]@example.com), numbers of 6+ digits become [number], and link query strings are removed.
    • Your API key is stored only on this computer (chrome.storage.local), never synced, never logged, and only sent to the provider in a request header.
    • Off the Hook only reads the email that is open. It never sends, deletes, moves, or edits email, and never clicks links.
    • The toolbar remembers only each tab's verdict and score (not email text) until the browser closes.
    • Your reports (sender, subject, link sites, your phrases and notes, and the email text only if you turn that on) stay on this computer unless you export them.

    Removes all settings, API keys, your reports, granted AI site permissions, and cached verdicts.

    Turn on AI analysis?

    With AI analysis on, the content of every email you open (sender, subject, body, links, attachment names) is sent to the AI provider you configure, after the privacy filtering you choose.

    That provider's privacy policy and data retention rules apply. For maximum privacy, use a local model with Ollama so nothing leaves your computer.

    Do not enable this for mailboxes your organization prohibits sharing with outside services.

    Clear all Off the Hook data?

    This resets every setting, deletes saved API keys and all your reports, and revokes AI site permissions. It cannot be undone.
