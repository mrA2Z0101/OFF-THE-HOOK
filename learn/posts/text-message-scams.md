Phishing by text message (sometimes called **smishing**) works because texts feel personal and urgent, and phones make it hard to see where a link really goes. The most common ones right now:

- **Unpaid tolls**: "You have an outstanding toll of $4.35. Pay now to avoid a $50 late fee." The FBI has warned about these.
- **Package problems**: "USPS: your package is held, confirm your address."
- **Bank fraud alerts**: "Did you approve a $900 charge? Reply NO or call this number."
- **Wrong number**: "Hi, is this Jessica?" A friendly chat that slowly turns into an investment scam.

```email
From: Text message from +1 (555) 013-2291
Subject: (text message)
Verdict: phishing

==TOLL SERVICES: You have an unpaid toll balance of $4.35.|Small amounts make you less careful==

==Pay by today to avoid a $50 late fee and suspension of your license.|Threats and a deadline==

==https://tollpay-services.top/pay|Not your toll agency's real website==
```

## How to handle texts

- **Don't tap the link.** Go to the toll agency's, carrier's or bank's site or app yourself.
- **Call the number on the back of your card** for bank alerts, not the number in the text.
- **Don't reply**, even "STOP", to texts you don't recognize. It tells them your number is active.
- **Report it**: on most phones you can forward scam texts to **7726** (SPAM), then delete them.

```quiz
A text says your package is held and asks you to confirm your address and pay a $1.99 redelivery fee. What's the best move?
- [ ] Pay it: it's only $1.99
- [x] Check your orders on the store's or carrier's real site yourself
- [ ] Reply STOP so they don't text again
Why: The small fee is to get your card number. Checking the real site tells you if there's actually a problem.
```
