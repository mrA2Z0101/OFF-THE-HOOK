In construction, opening drawings from people you don't know yet is part of the job. Scammers know that. A fake **invitation to bid** looks just like the real thing: a project name, a bid date, and a button to view the plans and specs.

The button goes to a page that looks like Microsoft, SharePoint or Dropbox and asks you to **sign in to see the files**. That's the whole trick: it's your email password they want.

```email
From: Estimating Dept <bids@ridgeline-precon.com>
To: you@yourcompany.com
Subject: ITB: Warehouse Expansion, Bid Due 11/14
Verdict: phishing

Good afternoon,

You are invited to submit a bid for structural and miscellaneous steel on the above project. Bid documents are available through our secure portal.

==VIEW PLANS & SPECS|The button goes to ridgeline-docs.sharepoint-files.net, which isn't SharePoint at all==

==Please sign in with your company email to access the files.|Real plan rooms don't need your email password==

==Bids are due Friday; drawings will only be available for 48 hours.|A rush so you don't stop to check==
```

## How to check

- **Hover the button.** Real plan rooms have their own sites. A "SharePoint" link that doesn't end in `sharepoint.com` is fake.
- **Never type your email password** to view files someone sent you. Real file shares either open directly or ask you to create an account with the plan room itself.
- **Look the company up yourself** and call the estimator on their published number.

```quiz
A bid invitation's "View Plans" button opens a page asking for your Microsoft 365 password. What does that tell you?
- [ ] It's normal: the files are protected
- [x] It's almost certainly phishing: plan rooms don't need your email password
- [ ] Try your password once to see what happens
Why: Asking for your own email password to see someone else's files is the giveaway. Close the page and contact the company another way.
```
