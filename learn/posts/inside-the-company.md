---
title: "It came from inside the company" isn't proof
date: 2026-10-20
summary: Hacked coworker accounts send the most convincing phishing of all. An unusual request from a real address still needs a real check.
tags: coworkers, basics
---

When an email comes from a coworker's **real** address, every normal check passes: the domain is right, the name is right, maybe it's even a reply in an old thread. That's exactly why scammers who break into one account immediately use it to email everyone else.

Typical signs that a coworker's account has been taken over:

- A **"please review this document"** email with a sign-in link, sent to lots of people at once
- A request that's **out of character**: gift cards, your cell number, a payment, a password
- **Odd timing or tone**: a message at 3 AM, or a coworker who's usually chatty writing one stiff line

```email
From: Dana Lee <dana.lee@yourcompany.com>
To: you@yourcompany.com
Subject: Shared: Q4 Schedule Updates
Verdict: phishing

Hi,

==I've shared a document with you. Please review and sign in to access.|Coworkers share files in your normal system, without a separate sign-in page==

==Open document|Goes to a non-Microsoft site that asks for your password==

Dana
```

## What to do

- **Check unusual requests in person**, by phone, or by a Teams message you start yourself.
- **Don't reply** to the suspicious email to ask if it's real; the scammer is reading it.
- **Tell IT**. Your coworker probably has no idea their account was hacked, and IT can stop it from spreading.

> [!NOTE] Off the Hook warns about this
> When an email from your own company asks for something sensitive, it shows a **Possibly compromised coworker account** warning and tells you to check with them in person.

```quiz
Your coworker's real email account sends you a link asking you to sign in to see a shared file. It's not like them. What do you do?
- [ ] Sign in: it's from their real address
- [ ] Reply and ask "Is this really you?"
- [x] Message or call them separately, and tell IT if they didn't send it
Why: A real address can still be a hacked account, and replies go to whoever controls it. A separate channel is the only real check.
```
