---
title: Sign-in prompts you didn't start
date: 2026-10-20
summary: If your phone keeps asking "Approve sign-in?" and you're not signing in, someone has your password. Deny it and tell IT.
tags: accounts, phone
---

Many work accounts use an app like **Microsoft Authenticator**: when you sign in, your phone asks you to approve it. It's a great protection, until someone gets you to approve **their** sign-in.

The trick is simple: a scammer who already has your password tries to sign in again and again. Your phone buzzes with approval requests, sometimes late at night, until you tap **Approve** just to make it stop. Some scammers also message or call pretending to be IT: "We're fixing your account, just approve the prompt."

> [!REDFLAG] Didn't start it? Don't approve it.
> An approval request you didn't trigger means someone else has your password and is trying to get in **right now**.

## What to do

1. **Deny** the request (or tap "No, it's not me" if your app offers it).
2. **Change your password** from a device you trust.
3. **Tell IT**, even if it stopped. They need to know your password is out there.

> [!NOTE] Number matching helps
> Many sign-in apps now ask you to type a number shown on the sign-in screen. If you aren't looking at a sign-in screen, there's no number to type, which is your clue that it's not you.

```quiz
It's 11 PM and your phone keeps showing "Approve sign-in request?" for your work account. You're not signing in. What do you do?
- [ ] Approve it once to make it stop
- [ ] Ignore it and deal with it in the morning
- [x] Deny it, change your password, and tell IT
Why: Someone has your password and is trying to get in now. Denying blocks them; changing your password and telling IT keeps them out.
```
