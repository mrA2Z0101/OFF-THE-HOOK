---
title: Why scammers love gift cards
date: 2026-10-20
summary: No real business, boss or government office pays with gift cards. The moment someone asks for the codes, you know it's a scam.
tags: money, coworkers
---

Gift cards are a scammer's favorite payment: once you read them the codes, the money is gone in minutes, it's nearly impossible to trace, and it can't be reversed.

That's why the gift card request shows up in so many scams:

- The **"boss"** who needs cards for a client and is stuck in a meeting
- The **"IRS" or "police"** saying you'll be arrested unless you pay
- The **"tech support"** agent who needs a fee to fix your computer
- The **"vendor"** offering a discount for gift-card payment

```email
From: Michael Torres <m.torres.ceo@gmail.com>
To: you@yourcompany.com
Subject: Quick favor
Verdict: phishing

Are you at your desk? ==I'm stuck in meetings all day|A reason you can't call to check==. ==I need you to pick up 5 Apple gift cards, $200 each, for a client.|The ask itself is the scam==

==Scratch the backs and email me photos of the codes.|Once they have the codes, the money is gone==

==Keep this between us for now, it's a surprise.|Secrecy stops you from checking==
```

> [!REDFLAG] The rule
> Anyone who asks you to **pay with gift cards** or **send photos of gift card codes** is a scammer. No exceptions.

```quiz
Which of these is a normal business request?
- [ ] Your "CEO" asks for $1,000 in gift cards for a client
- [ ] A "vendor" offers 10% off if you pay with gift cards
- [x] HR asks you to fill in your benefits form in the HR system you already use
Why: The first two are gift-card scams. Real requests use your normal systems, not gift card codes sent by email.
```
