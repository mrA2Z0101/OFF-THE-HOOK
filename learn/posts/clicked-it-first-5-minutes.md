title: Clicked it? Your first 5 minutes

It happens to careful people too: a busy morning, a convincing email, one click. The click is rarely the disaster. **Waiting and hoping** is.

## If you typed your password

1. **Change it right away**, from the real site (type the address yourself) or from another device.
2. If you use that password anywhere else, change it there too.
3. **Tell IT now.** They can sign the scammer out of your account and check what they touched.

## If you opened a file or installed something

1. **Disconnect**: turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the network cable. Don't shut down; IT may need to look at it.
2. **Call IT** (don't email from that computer).

## If you sent money or information

1. Call your bank or accounting **immediately**. Payments can sometimes be stopped if you're fast.
2. Tell IT and your manager.

> [!TIP] Don't delete the email
> IT needs it to block the sender and warn everyone else who got it. Report it, then leave it alone.

> [!NOTE] No blame
> People who report fast save companies a lot of money. People who hide it out of embarrassment are how small problems become big ones.

```quiz
You clicked a link in a "Microsoft" email and typed your password before you realized the site was fake. What should you do first?
- [ ] Delete the email so nobody knows
- [ ] Wait to see if anything strange happens
- [x] Change your password from the real site and tell IT right away
Why: The scammer may already be trying your password. Changing it and telling IT quickly can lock them out before they do damage.
```
