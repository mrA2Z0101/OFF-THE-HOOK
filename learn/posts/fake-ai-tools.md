Everyone wants to try the newest AI tool, and scammers are happy to "help". Watch for:

- **Free premium upgrades**: "Your ChatGPT Plus trial is ready, sign in with Microsoft to activate."
- **AI browser extensions** that promise to summarize your email, but read everything on every page, including your passwords.
- **Fake AI apps** in search ads that look like the real one but are copies.

```email
From: OpenAI Team <promo@chatgpt-plus-offers.com>
To: you@yourcompany.com
Subject: Your free ChatGPT Plus upgrade is waiting
Verdict: phishing

Congratulations! Your company qualifies for ==12 months of ChatGPT Plus at no cost|Too good to be true, and not from openai.com==.

To activate, ==sign in with your Microsoft work account|An AI promo has no reason to need your work password==.

==Activate now==

This offer expires in 24 hours.
```

## Before you try a new AI tool

- **Go to the company's site yourself.** Don't use links from emails or ads.
- **Ask IT first** before connecting any AI tool to your work email, files or calendar. Some tools can read everything they're given access to.
- **Install add-ons only from the official store**, and check how many people use them and who publishes them.
- Never paste passwords, customer details or confidential drawings into an AI tool your company hasn't approved.

```quiz
A browser extension promises to "summarize every email with AI" and asks for permission to "read and change all your data on all websites". What should you do?
- [ ] Install it: it's in the browser store, so it's safe
- [x] Ask IT first: that permission lets it read everything you see
- [ ] Install it and uninstall it later if it's bad
Why: "All your data on all websites" includes your email and every password you type. Approved tools only.
```
