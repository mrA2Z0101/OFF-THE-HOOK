Fake recruiters are everywhere on LinkedIn and job sites, and AI makes their profiles, job posts and messages look completely professional. They're after your **money**, your **personal information** (for identity theft), or a way **into your company**.

Warning signs:

- The offer is **too good**: high pay, fully remote, almost no experience needed.
- They want to **move the chat** to WhatsApp, Telegram or text right away.
- There's **no real interview**, just a chat, or an "interview" over text.
- They ask for your **Social Security number, a photo of your ID, or bank details** before you've been hired.
- You have to **pay** for training, equipment or a "background check", or deposit a check and send some of it back.

```email
From: Rachel Kim, Talent Acquisition <rachel.kim.hr@gmail.com>
To: you@personal-email.com
Subject: Remote Project Coordinator role - $48/hr
Verdict: phishing

Hi! I came across your profile on LinkedIn and ==you're a perfect fit for a fully remote Project Coordinator role at $48/hour.|Flattery plus a very high offer==

==Please add me on WhatsApp so we can do a quick text interview.|Moving you off the platform where scams get reported==

==To start onboarding, send a photo of your driver's license and your SSN.|No real employer needs this before hiring you==
```

## How to check

- **Look up the company yourself** and apply through its real careers page.
- **Check the recruiter**: a real one uses a company email address, not Gmail, and has a history.
- **Never pay** to get a job, and never send ID or bank details before a written offer you've verified.

```quiz
A recruiter offers you a job after a short WhatsApp chat and asks for your SSN to "start payroll". What's the right move?
- [ ] Send it: they need it to pay you
- [x] Stop and verify the company and the offer through their official website first
- [ ] Send only the last four digits
Why: Real employers collect that information through official onboarding after a verified offer. Asking for it in a chat is the scam.
```
